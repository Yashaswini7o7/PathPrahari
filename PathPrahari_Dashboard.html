What you are looking at

This page is generated by run_demo.py. Every number on it comes from the model run on your machine: the route options for 3 trips × 24 departure hours × 3 weather conditions are computed in Python and stored inside this file, so it works offline.

About the data

Road features follow the schema of open datasets: OpenStreetMap for road geometry, curvature and junctions; NASA VIIRS night lights for street lighting; SRTM for gradients; MoRTH blackspot lists and Open-Meteo/IMD weather. In this prototype the road network itself is a generated corridor with that same schema, placed at Varanasi–Prayagraj belt coordinates, so everything runs without downloads.

There is no public Indian dataset of fleet harsh-braking events with GPS positions, so 12 months of fleet telematics are simulated (180 trucks). The simulation includes patterns reported in MoRTH data and common on Indian highways: higher risk at night, the 2–5 AM fatigue window, unlit stretches, winter fog, monsoon rain, busy junctions, plus hidden hotspots (median cuts, truck parking near dhabas) that road features alone cannot reveal. A real fleet's GPS export replaces the simulator without changing anything else.

How to read the risk numbers

Risk is the expected number of incidents per 10,000 trips on that route at that departure time and weather. The range next to it is a 90% interval: when data on a road is thin, the range is wide and the confidence label drops to Medium or Low.

Hotspots are ranked by risk per vehicle passing, not by raw counts, so a busy highway does not look dangerous just because it is busy.
